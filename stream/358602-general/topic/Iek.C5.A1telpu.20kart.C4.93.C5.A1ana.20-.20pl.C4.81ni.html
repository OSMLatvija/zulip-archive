kaposts (Oct 06 2026 at 18:04):

@markalex With regards to Origo. I was making an attempt at that as well. But if you were working on that as well we should better coordinate our work so that there aren't too much conflicts. Had some fun figuring out all the level=-1 connections.





Didn't do any direct tracing over the plans published in Origo website (or other Centrālā stacija plans which can be found online) since those plans aren't published using suitable license. Only careful note taking and deriving positions from satellite pictures, and the 2x2 grid which is more or less used by middle section. If you start with windows there are 15 of them 4m wide each and make a grid. The major columns and walls more or less aligned with this grid and the shops are between columns. Ceiling windows floor outlines are also clues, recently finished 3d parts and almost every floor in Origo has unique shape.

As for ground level differences osm wiki for simple indoor is quite clear that level=0 should be lowest one which has access to street level. As for the part which connects to platform there is no reason why outdoor paths (on the platform) can't connect to doors/indoor corridors at level=1/2. Another interesting aspect is level height mismatch between Stacija and mall buildings. Floors in Origo section are slightly taller than Stacija, the platform connects to level=2 in Stacija, but level=1 in Origo side. Inside level=1 stacija connects to level=1 in Origo.

Corridors/multipolygons and island shops. I would say -> just map the majority of island shops as point features. I have even seen some people argue that regular shops (indoor or not) should also be mapped as point features inside rooms/buildings, not directly on rooms since the shops change more frequently than rooms. Instead of messing with multipolygons there is always an option split them into hole free shapes. 2 side by side indoor=area or indoor=corridor have no walls in between so it shouldn't be an issue for rendering or routing. And logically from the perspective of how you would describe it in common language corridor is usually a straight thing so splitting larger floors into multiple corridors wouldn't be so bad. It has additional side benefit of minimizing impact of changes -> when shops get merged or split into smaller ones only connected corridor would be modified instead of whole floor.
